Trust and compliance

Handling protected health information is the boring part. We take it seriously anyway.

Every claim we touch carries PHI. That means the controls around it matter as much as the throughput. Here is what is audited, what is encrypted, who can see what, and what happens if something goes wrong.

Current

HIPAA

Security, Privacy, and Breach Notification Rules. A Business Associate Agreement is signed before a single record moves.

Type II

SOC 2 Type II

Security, availability, and confidentiality controls tested by an independent auditor across a full 12-month window.

r2 certified

HITRUST CSF

The healthcare-specific framework that maps HIPAA, NIST, and ISO into one certifiable set of controls.

5010

X12 5010

Certified against the HIPAA-mandated transaction formats for eligibility, claims, status, and remittance.

Encryption and data handling

PHI is encrypted before it leaves your network and stays encrypted at rest. It never appears anywhere it can leak.

  • TLS 1.3 in transit, AES-256 at rest. No unencrypted transport is accepted, including from legacy practice management systems.
  • Keys held in a managed HSM with scheduled rotation and separation from the systems that use them.
  • No PHI in URLs, logs, or error reports. Identifiers are tokenized before anything reaches observability tooling.
  • US-only data residency. Storage and processing stay within US regions, including backups.

Access control

The smallest number of people who can do the job, for the shortest time they need it.

  • SSO and enforced MFA on every account. SAML and SCIM provisioning so offboarding is immediate.
  • Role-based least privilege. Billing staff, providers, and administrators each see only the records their role requires.
  • Just-in-time production access. Engineer access is time-boxed, approved, and expires automatically.
  • Quarterly access reviews with an exportable record for your own audits.

Audit trail

Every read and write against a record is logged, immutably, with enough context to answer an OCR request.

  • Actor, timestamp, record, and reason captured on every PHI access — including our own staff.
  • Write-once retention for seven years, beyond the six-year HIPAA documentation requirement.
  • Self-serve export. Pull a full access history for any patient or date range without opening a ticket.
  • Anomaly alerts on unusual volume, off-hours access, or bulk record retrieval.

Vendors and BAAs

Your obligations do not stop at our door, so neither do ours. Every downstream party is under contract.

  • A signed BAA with every customer and with every subprocessor that could touch PHI.
  • Published subprocessor list with advance notice before anyone is added.
  • Annual vendor security review, including clearinghouse and payer connectivity partners.
  • Contractual deletion on exit. Your data is returned or destroyed on a defined schedule, with certification.

Incident response

Rehearsed, documented, and time-bound. You hear from a person, not a status page.

  • 24/7 monitoring with on-call escalation and defined severity tiers.
  • Customer notice within 72 hours of confirming an incident affecting your data — well inside HIPAA's 60-day breach notification window.
  • Annual tabletop exercises and a post-incident report shared with affected customers.
  • Tested disaster recovery with a one-hour recovery point and four-hour recovery time objective.
